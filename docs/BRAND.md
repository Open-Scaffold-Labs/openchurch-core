## Founding Decisions

*Recorded at adoption, so that "why is the brand this way?" is never answered with
"because we liked it."*

1. **The Open Mark was chosen because it felt inevitable, not trendy.** Alternatives that
   felt clever or contemporary were rejected on those exact grounds. The approved monogram
   reads as something *found* — as if it had always existed in the Church's visual world
   and was merely uncovered — which is precisely the feeling OpenChurch's custody promise
   requires.

2. **The identity draws from ecclesiastical architecture, not contemporary SaaS branding.**
   The Mark's construction language is the spire, the finial, and the illuminated capital —
   not the rounded-sans bubble letter. The reference library for this identity is a
   cathedral close and a sacristy drawer, never a startup's landing page.

3. **Physical craftsmanship was treated as equal to digital interfaces from day one.**
   The Mark was approved simultaneously as an app icon, a blind emboss, a wax seal, a foil
   stamp, and a brass plaque. This was deliberate: OpenChurch's constitution holds that
   artifacts outlive dashboards (P7), so the identity was *born* physical rather than
   adapted to the physical later. Every future reproduction question inherits this stance.

4. **The guiding principle became: "Honor tradition. Protect it with modern tools."**
   The brand does not modernize the Church. It modernizes administration while honoring
   tradition. Technology exists to preserve ministry — not replace it.

5. **The system was designed to feel like it could have belonged to the Church centuries
   ago while functioning naturally on modern devices.** This is the identity's acceptance
   test, formalized below as the Four Surfaces Test. Both halves are binding: a mark that
   only works embossed is heritage cosplay; a mark that only works on screens is a tech
   logo. The Open Mark was approved because it passes both without strain.

6. **The words were fixed with the image.** The tagline is **"Stewardship. Order.
   Worship."** — three plain nouns, in the office's own register, each earned by the
   product (the ledger, the packet, the planner). The meaning of the Mark is fixed as:
   *the O for Open, the C for Church — intertwined, enduring, open.* The supporting
   language — "Open to all. Rooted in tradition. Built for ministry. Here for
   generations." and "Timeless by Design. Trusted for Generations." — is canon and may be
   quoted verbatim wherever the brand speaks for itself.

## 1. The Open Mark

### 1.1 What it is

An intertwined **O/C monogram** crowned and footed with **spire finials** — the pointed,
diamond-tipped verticals of ecclesiastical architecture. The O (Open) threads through the
C (Church): two letters, one continuous gesture. Together — one mission, one future.

The symbolism is fixed on the finalist sheet, part by part, and is quotable as canon:

- **The O — Open.** Openness. Welcome. Community. A door to ministry and mission.
- **The C — Church.** The Church. Our calling. Our shared purpose.
- **The spire — Together.** United in mission. Built to last. Supporting what matters.

The selection-stage rationale (Finalist A1) recorded five reasons the Mark won, preserved
verbatim: timeless and enduring · directly represents OpenChurch · scales beautifully
across every medium · **feels ecclesiastical without being denominational** · memorable,
elegant, and uniquely ours. The fourth reason carries doctrine: the Mark must serve
Episcopal, Catholic, and Lutheran parishes with equal ease (Manual §18, "not a pulpit") —
which is why it is architecture, not iconography. It quotes the spire, never the crucifix.

### 1.2 Why it works — the documented reasoning

**It is a monogram, and monograms are the Church's own genre.** Ecclesiastical visual
history is full of interlaced letterforms — the Chi-Rho, the IHS, the Marian monogram,
carved keystones and embroidered vestments. An interlaced O/C does not *borrow* reverence;
it works in a form the audience's eye already trusts. A parish secretary has seen this
species of mark her whole life — on cornerstone, chalice, and certificate — which is why
it reads instantly as institution rather than app.

**The finials do the architecture's work.** The vertical spires give the Mark aspiration
(it points upward, as the buildings do), axis (a spine of symmetry that makes it feel
engineered, like a keystone), and age (the diamond-tipped finial is a centuries-old
detail; nothing about it can go out of fashion because it was never *in* fashion).

**The intertwining is the message.** Open and Church are not adjacent; they pass through
each other. That is the whole thesis of the product — openness *inside* the institution,
modern tools *inside* the tradition — drawn as a single figure. The Mark communicates:
openness, stewardship, continuity, permanence, trust.

**It survives poverty.** Stripped to one color, cut into brass, pressed blind into
leather with no ink at all — the Mark's identity lives entirely in its silhouette and
line-weight contrast. Identity that depends on color, gradient, or effect dies at the
engraver's bench. This one was approved *because* it doesn't.

### 1.3 The Four Surfaces Test (binding)

Every reproduction, revision of this book, or new application must keep the Mark equally
natural on all four canonical surfaces:

1. **Embossed in leather** — no ink, pure relief.
2. **Engraved in brass** — one material, one depth.
3. **Printed on a certificate** — one color, fine paper, formal company.
4. **Displayed on a phone** — the app icon at thumbnail size.

Anything that passes only some of these — an effect, a gradient, a thin flourish that
clogs at 16px or vanishes in relief — is not the Mark.

### 1.4 Do not redesign

The symbol is final. It is not redrawn, reweighted, italicized, ornamented, simplified,
animated into a new shape, or "refreshed." It may be *re-mastered* (vector cleanup,
optical corrections at small sizes) by explicit creative-lead decision, recorded here with
a date and a reason — the same amendment discipline as the Constitution.

## 2. Lockups & Variants

**Primary mark** — the monogram alone. Use when the name is already established or the
surface is intimate (icon, seal, spine, plaque).

**Horizontal lockup** — monogram left, OPENCHURCH wordmark right, tagline beneath the
wordmark. The workhorse: website header, letterhead, documentation.

**Vertical lockup** — monogram above, wordmark beneath, tagline last. For centered,
ceremonial compositions: title pages, certificates, signage.

**Centered lockup** — monogram above wordmark, no tagline. For tight spaces that still
deserve formality.

**Wordmark** — OPENCHURCH set in the display serif, generously letterspaced, with the
small-caps voice of an engraving. Never set the name in the body sans in brand contexts.

**Tagline** — STEWARDSHIP. ORDER. WORSHIP. Letterspaced small caps, brass or ink, always
subordinate in size to the wordmark. The tagline never appears without the name.

### Approved color variants

| Variant | Use |
|---|---|
| **Church Burgundy** | The default. Print, digital, signage. |
| **Ink Black** | One-color reproduction, documents, fax-grade contexts. |
| **Stone Gray** | Quiet contexts: footers, watermarks, secondary pages. |
| **Brass Foil** | Honor contexts: foil stamping, certificates, seals, plaques. |
| *(Reversed: Parchment on burgundy)* | Dark grounds — the app icon is the canonical example. |

The finalist sheet additionally approves the strict black-and-white set — black-on-white,
reversed white-on-black — for contexts with no color at all (photocopy, newsprint, laser
letterhead). The Mark must always survive a parish's office printer; that, too, is the
Four Surfaces Test in street clothes.

Brass foil is the *ceremonial* variant — reserved for artifacts of recognition and record.
If everything is foil, nothing is.

## 3. Space, Scale & Misuse

### 3.1 Clearspace

The Mark's exclusion zone on every side is the height of its own finial (the diamond-tipped
spire unit). Nothing enters it: no type, no rules, no page edge, no parish seal. The
finial is part of the Mark; crowding it amputates the architecture.

### 3.2 Minimum sizes

- **Digital:** the finalist sheet fixes the canonical specimen sizes — **16 / 32 / 64 /
  256px** — and the Mark must hold at all four. 16px is the floor (favicon); prefer 24px+.
  Lockups never below the size at which the tagline is legible — drop the tagline before
  shrinking it into dirt.
- **Print:** monogram 10mm; horizontal lockup 30mm wide.
- **Relief (emboss/engrave/wax):** monogram 15mm — below this, the intertwine closes up
  in physical media. When in doubt, strike a sample.

### 3.3 Misuse (reject on sight)

Never: stretch, squash, rotate, or tilt the Mark · add gradients, glows, bevels, or
drop-shadows · outline the filled form or fill the engraved form · recolor outside the
five approved variants · place it on busy photography without a solid ground · intertwine
it with another logo · animate it beyond a calm fade · use the O or the C alone · redraw
the finials · set the wordmark in another face · attach it to content the Constitution
bans. The Mark on a parish's artifact follows the Manual's rule: **the parish's name is
the headline; ours is the colophon.**

## 4. Reproduction Standards

**Print (offset/digital).** One-color reproduction is the default dignity: Church
Burgundy or Ink Black on parchment-toned stock. Coated stocks are tolerated; uncoated,
toothy, archival cotton is the brand's home. Certificates and recognition materials use
cotton paper — they are meant to be kept.

**Foil stamping.** Warm brass foil, matte or satin — never mirror-chrome, never
holographic. Foil the monogram or the full lockup; never foil body text.

**Blind emboss.** The most reverent reproduction: no ink, pure relief on heavy stock or
leather. Use the monogram alone. Depth over drama — a whisper you can feel.

**Engraving (brass, stone).** Single depth, the approved silhouette only. Brushed or
patinated brass; polished mirror finishes read corporate-lobby, not parish-door.

**Wax seal.** Burgundy wax, monogram die, 25–30mm. For ceremony and delight: founding
parish welcomes, milestone documents. Never required, always kept simple.

**Digital.** The app icon is canon: brass monogram reversed on Church Burgundy field,
softly rounded square, **no gloss, no gradient lighting, no long-shadow** — flat, warm,
and still. Favicon: monogram alone. In-product, the Mark appears once per surface at most
(masthead *or* colophon) — Principle 9 applies to our own logo first.

## 7. Typography

**Display: Cormorant Garamond** — headings, the wordmark, canonical figures, everything
engraved or kept. Chosen for cathedral proportions: high contrast, sharp serifs, the
letterforms of an illuminated manuscript's descendants.

**Body & UI: Source Sans Pro** — working text, controls, documentation. Chosen because it
is humanist, legible, unassuming, and free to every parish forever — the body face is
plumbing, and plumbing should be dependable and quiet. *(See §6.2: the shipped UI
currently uses Inter in this role; same doctrine, pending ratification.)*

The pairing enacts the Manual's rule: **serif for what the Church keeps, sans for what
the office does.** Brand contexts add one register: letterspaced small caps (either face)
for labels and taglines — the typography of engraving. Never a third typeface; never a
novelty face; never the name in the sans.

## 10. Construction & Production Vector

The Mark's construction is specified on the sheet committed beside this file —
`docs/brand/openmark-construction-2025.png` ("The Open Mark", v1.0). The binding rules:

- **Construction grid (A–E):** A overall height · B overall width · C vertical spine ·
  D crossbar height · E stem thickness. The O and C share a common optical center; the
  spine is centered and extends past the forms into finials to evoke a cross; stroke
  thickness (E) is consistent throughout; the inner counter stays open.
- **Optical balance:** top and bottom finials are optically aligned; the crossbar is
  raised slightly to optically center on the O; curves are corrected optically, not
  mathematically.
- **Clear space:** minimum clear space on every side = X, where X is the thickness of
  the vertical spine.
- **Minimum size:** 24px digital / 12mm print; below that, use the simplified mark.
- **Improper usage:** do not stretch/distort, recolor outside the palette, add
  shadows/effects, rotate, or crowd the mark.

**Production vector.** The master was vectorized from the sheet's black-and-white
specimen and lives at `docs/brand/openmark.svg` (single path, `currentColor` — one file
drives every color variant), with `openmark_gold.svg` and `openmark_burgundy.svg`
alongside. In the product it ships as `client/src/components/OpenMark.jsx`; the favicon is
`client/public/icon.svg` and the iOS icon `client/public/apple-touch-icon.png` (gold on
Church Burgundy). *Follow-up: a chunkier simplified mark for ≤16px favicon use.*
