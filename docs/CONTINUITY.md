# The OpenChurch Continuity Promise

_This page is written to be published (site footer: "Your data & continuity")
once reviewed. It answers the question every rector rightly asks a
one-person software company: **"What happens to our records if something
happens to you?"**_

---

Your parish's records belong to your parish. Not to OpenChurch, not to a
vendor, not to a database. That principle is built into the software in ways
you can verify yourself:

**You can leave any time, with everything.** Settings → Your data →
*Download parish export* gives any parish admin a complete copy of every
record — the sacramental register, Mass intentions, members, giving history,
attendance, everything — in an open, documented format (JSON) that any
technician can read and any future system can import. No request, no
waiting, no exit interview.

**Your records are backed up beyond us.** Every week, an encrypted-transport
copy of your parish's data is exported and stored off-platform, in addition
to the database's own daily backups and point-in-time recovery.

**If OpenChurch ever winds down:** you receive a minimum of **90 days'
written notice**, your subscription fees stop immediately, the export stays
available for the entire notice period, and we will personally help every
parish move its register — including generating printed archival copies of
the full register for the parish safe, at no cost. This commitment is part
of our terms of service, not a blog post.

**The register is paper-compatible on purpose.** Certificates, sacristy
sheets, and register archives are designed to print beautifully, because the
century view matters: whatever happens to any software company — ours
included — a parish that uses OpenChurch can always put its memory back on
paper, in a form worthy of the book it came from.

One more honest sentence, because parishes deserve it: OpenChurch is built
and kept by one person, with the care that implies — and these mechanisms
exist precisely so that your parish's continuity never depends on any one
person. Including me.

— Jose Rivera, OpenChurch
